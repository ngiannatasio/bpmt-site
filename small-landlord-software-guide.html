Property Management Software for Landlords With Under 10 Units

Written by an active real estate operator · Last updated Oct 2026

At this size, the biggest mistake is paying for software built for someone else. You need rent to land in your account on time, a clean way to screen applicants, and a place for tenants to send maintenance requests. Almost everything else is a nice-to-have.

The short version

One to three units, minimal bookkeeping: start with a free landlord tool such as Avail or TurboTenant. Four to ten units, or you want real accounting in one place: DoorLoop. Revisit the decision when you cross roughly 20 units.

What you actually need at this size

  1. Online rent collection. ACH into your bank account, automatic reminders, and a record of who paid when. This alone replaces most of the chasing.
  2. Tenant screening and applications. Credit, background and eviction checks you can run without a phone call. Check who pays for the report — you or the applicant.
  3. A maintenance request inbox. Tenants submit with photos; you have a dated trail if something becomes a dispute.
  4. Leases and e-signatures. State-specific lease templates are a real benefit if you own in a heavily regulated state.
  5. Bookkeeping you will actually keep up with. Some landlords are fine exporting to a spreadsheet or their accountant; others want income and expenses tracked per property inside the tool.

Free tools: Avail and TurboTenant

Both run a free tier aimed squarely at small landlords, covering listings, applications, screening and basic rent collection, and both sell a low-cost premium upgrade for extras like applicant-paid screening, custom lease options and fewer transaction fees. Avail leans into state-specific lease templates; TurboTenant leans into screening and its large small-landlord user base. The trade-off with free tiers is accounting: neither is trying to be your general ledger. Plans change often, so confirm current tiers on each site before you commit.

When paying for DoorLoop makes sense

Once you are past a handful of units, the hidden cost is your time reconciling payments, expenses and owner numbers across separate tools. DoorLoop puts rent collection, maintenance and full accounting in one place, and its onboarding is the easiest we have seen. What to skip

Buildium and AppFolio are strong products, but at under 10 units you would be paying for trust accounting, owner portals and reporting you will not use. AppFolio in particular is built for management companies and has unit minimums. See the three-way comparison for where they start to make sense.

The verdict

Start free if your portfolio is tiny and your bookkeeping is simple. Move to DoorLoop when the number of separate tools and spreadsheets starts costing you real time. Either way, pick based on the portfolio you expect to own in a year, because switching later means a migration.
