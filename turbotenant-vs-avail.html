TurboTenant vs Avail: Which Free Landlord Software Is Better?

Research-based comparison by an active real estate operator · Last updated Oct 2026

TurboTenant and Avail are the two best-known free tools for small landlords, and on the surface they look almost identical. The real differences are in how their paid plans are priced, who pays rent-payment fees, and how much lease help you get for free.

The short version

You own in a heavily regulated state, or want leases handled for free: Avail. You have more than a few units and want cheap, flat paid features: TurboTenant. You need real accounting: neither; look at DoorLoop.

Where Avail wins

Avail includes state-specific, lawyer-reviewed leases on its free plan, and its listings syndicate across the Realtor.com network. For a landlord with one or two units, Avail's free plan may be all you ever need. Read the Avail review →

Where TurboTenant wins

Cost at scale. TurboTenant's paid plans are flat annual prices, while Avail's upgrade is charged per unit. At five units, Avail Unlimited Plus is about $540 a year; TurboTenant Pro is $199. TurboTenant's Pro plan also includes tax-focused accounting. Read the TurboTenant review →

The verdict

Start on either free plan; you lose nothing by trying both. If you end up needing paid features across several units, TurboTenant is usually the cheaper upgrade. If leases in a strict state are your main worry, Avail is the safer default. And if bookkeeping has become a real chore, it is time for a full platform.
